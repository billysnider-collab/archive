### A. Strait of Hormuz — projectile strike on a commercial vessel

**What happened.** Early Sunday, Iranian state media said an Iranian commercial vessel was struck off Qeshm/Hengam, one crew dead, three to four wounded. UKMTO separately warned that a vessel transiting the strait was hit by a projectile and later reported a fire and evacuation. The US military had not commented as of this as-of time. **FACT.** (AP/Washington Post/CNN, reliability A, 13 Sep 2026; UKMTO via same wires, A, 13 Sep 2026)

**What we know.** Timing is ~0500 Iran time. Qeshm governor blamed a "terrorist enemy" and said the projectile type is unidentified. Iran and Oman had already agreed "details" of new entry/exit routes and planned to brief Gulf states in Muscat on Monday. Foreign Minister Araghchi said the Oman deal "does not in any way mean reopening" the strait. CNN cites traffic still ~90% below pre-war levels (pre-war ~20 mb/d through Hormuz). **FACT / estimate.** (IRNA/IRIB via AP, B for attribution / A for republication, 13 Sep; Tasnim/Iran Intl on Araghchi, B, 13 Sep; CNN traffic figure, B, 13 Sep)

**What we don't know.** Shooter, munition, whether UKMTO and the Iranian ship are the same hull, whether this is a US blockade action, an Iranian misfire, a proxy, or a third party. No CENTCOM confirmation. No independent imagery of the hull in this as-of window.

**Assessment.** **ASSESSMENT, MODERATE:** The strike is timed to poison or harden the Muscat briefing. Even a small hull fire in a strait already running at residual traffic keeps the war-risk premium bid. **INFERENCE, MODERATE:** If the shooter is the United States, the message is that Iranian-flagged logistics remain targetable during talks. If it is not, Tehran gains a grievance on the eve of a Gulf meeting.

**Decision points.** Monday Muscat attendance list (Bahrain has been reported as ruling itself out of at least one format). Whether CENTCOM issues a statement. Whether insurers widen exclusion zones.

**Indicators.** UKMTO/IMB follow-on warnings; AIS gaps around Qeshm–Larak; any named tanker fixture cancelled Monday; official Iranian casualty revision.

**Red-team view.** Alternative analyst: this is not a US shot. It is either an IRGC-adjacent mishap or a deliberate Iranian incident designed to freeze Muscat into a political communiqué without restoring transit — preserving leverage while blaming "terrorism." Confidence on the alternative: **LOW–MODERATE**. Evidence that would kill it: a CENTCOM or third-navy weapons-debris readout.

### B. Saudi East–West pipeline shut after drones from Iraq

**What happened.** Drones launched from Iraqi territory struck the East–West pipeline system in the Riyadh and Medina regions on 10–11 September. Saudi Arabia shut the line "as a precaution." Iraq dismissed the Maysan operations commander and says it seized a launch platform. Riyadh is holding fire after Baghdad asked for time. **FACT.** (Saudi MFA; BBC; Reuters; Bloomberg; Al Jazeera citing INA, reliability A/B, 11–13 Sep)

**What we know.** Several injuries, no confirmed deaths in official Saudi language. Pump stations were hit; satellite thermal detections and smoke plumes were reported along the route. Reuters/analysts have described the line as moving roughly 4–5% of global supply as a Hormuz workaround. **FACT / estimate.** (Reuters, A, 11–12 Sep)

**What we don't know.** Exact militia identity. Degree of IRGC direction versus local initiative. Repair timeline and residual throughput. Whether more launch cells remain in Maysan.

**Assessment.** **ASSESSMENT, HIGH:** Closing the bypass while Hormuz is constricted is the single largest physical energy event of the week. **ASSESSMENT, MODERATE:** Baghdad's commander firing is damage control, not proof of restored monopoly on force in Maysan.

**Decision points.** Saudi choice: accept an Iraqi internal clean-up, or strike militia infrastructure. Iraqi choice: confront Iran-aligned factions in an election-adjacent security climate.

**Indicators.** Official Saudi restart notice; additional FIRMS/thermal hits on pump stations; PMF or named militia statements; any Saudi request at the UN or GCC.

### C. Houthi seizure of Mocha and push onto Bab el-Mandeb

**What happened.** Houthi forces took the Red Sea port of Mocha on 10 September; government sources described a further push to Dhubab and claims of moves onto islands in the strait (Mayyun/Perim and Hanish appear in some government-source accounts). Saudi aircraft have struck Houthi areas; Houthis claim 129 Saudi sorties in 48 hours (Houthi figure — interested party). Houthis also say international shipping through Bab al-Mandab remains "near normal." **FACT as to Mocha; UNCONFIRMED as to full island control.** (Reuters, Al Jazeera, WSJ, reliability A/B, 10–13 Sep; Houthi claim, C, 13 Sep)

**What we know.** Reuters, citing Yemeni government, Iranian, and regional sources, reported IRGC guidance and arms in the coastal offensive. UN has warned of humanitarian reversal. Displaced civilians are moving toward Aden/Lahj.

**What we don't know.** Durable control of Perim/Mayyun. Whether Houthis will now impose a formal inspection/toll regime. Whether the US will treat the advance as a Red Sea war problem or an Iran-war adjunct.

**Assessment.** **ASSESSMENT, HIGH:** Mocha is a real loss for the Presidential Leadership Council and tightens Houthi geography on the second global energy gate. **ASSESSMENT, MODERATE:** "Control of the strait" is being over-claimed in some headlines; coastal seizure plus island claims is not yet a demonstrated, persistent interdiction campaign on the scale of 2023–24, but the option is now physically closer.

**Decision points.** Saudi air-land response vs. limited punishment. US choice on whether Houthi outreach to Washington (as described by Trump in secondary reporting) buys restraint.

**Indicators.** Commercial AIS deviations at Bab el-Mandeb; confirmed imagery of Houthi presence on Perim; insurance circulars; Aden government counterattack.
