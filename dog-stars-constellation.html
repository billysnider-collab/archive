Nine Years Out

How the World Ended

Not one collapse but three, stacked on top of each other — a virus, a second sickness born from the first, and a planet that was already dying underneath it all.

Stage One — The Flu
A hyper-virulent, airborne influenza strain kills — by Hig's count — "ninety nine point whatever" percent of the population in a matter of weeks. The press blamed New Delhi; Cima, the epidemiologist, has heard the other rumor — a genetic accident at the Livermore weapons lab, a courier's plane down in Brampton. No one ever confirmed either story.
Stage Two — The Blood Disease
Survivors of the flu develop a slow autoimmune condition, "something like AIDS" — bruising, hemorrhage, organ damage, a body turning on itself. Children born after the pandemic inherit it. There is no cure, only Cima's guess that vitamin D deficiency speeds it along.
Stage Three — The Land Itself
Underneath both plagues, the climate was already failing: warming streams killed the trout, drought and beetles killed the forests, snowpack thinned until whole canyons dried up. Elk, geese, tigers, apes — gone. Only the adaptable survive: carp, coyotes, raptors, and the people still stubborn enough to farm.
Why Survivors Turned Feral
No law, no courts, no hospitals — just scarcity and the people it makes of us. Bangley's doctrine, "New world now. New world new rules," is the logic almost everyone left alive has adopted: the men at the Coke truck, the marauders on the plains, the couple at Grand Junction who rigged a radio beacon to lure pilots into a cable trap. Trust became a luxury no one could afford.
Constellations

Who Endures

The survivors — each a star in a broken sky, bound by grief, love, and the stubborn will to keep going.

✈︎
Hig
Pilot · Narrator
A poet, carpenter, and reluctant killer. He flies the Beast, tends his garden, and carries the weight of a world he couldn't save.
I keep the Beast running, I keep the 100 low lead on tap, I foresee attacks. I am young enough, I am old enough.
⚔
Bangley
Survivalist · Protector
Ruthless, pragmatic, and utterly alone. He lives for the perimeter, kills without hesitation, and guards Hig like a difficult brother.
Never ever negotiate. You are negotiating your own death.
🐾
Jasper
Copilot · Heart
Hig's blue heeler, his last tether to tenderness. He dies in the mountains, leaving Hig with nothing but the stars.
Goodbye, bud. You are Jasper. My heart. We are never apart, not here, not there.
🌿
Cima
Doctor · Healer
A survivor who carries her own losses — a twin brother, a husband she had to kill. She meets Hig in their shared brokenness.
If you want, you can just look at me. It's probably been a long time. I'm in no hurry.
🕊
Melissa
Memory · Love
Hig's wife, lost to the flu. He smothered her at her request — the act that haunts him more than any other.
I loved her. This is what I live with.
🏔
Pops
Rancher · Father
Cima's father, a former Navy SEAL who carries the old West in his bones. Dignified, weary, and quietly hopeful.
The green patches are spreading year by year.
The Arc of the Sky

Themes that Burn

Loss, memory, violence, hope — the novel's constellations are carved from what remains when civilization falls away.

Grief as an Element
“Grief is an element. It has its own cycle like the carbon cycle, the nitrogen. It never diminishes not ever.”

Hig carries grief the way the earth carries water — it cycles through him, never disappearing, only changing form.
Survival vs. Living
Bangley survives. Hig lives.

The novel asks: what are we waiting for? Mere survival is not enough — we need love, beauty, connection. The risk is everything.
The Collapse of Nature
“If I ever woke up crying in the middle of a dream… it's because the trout are gone every one.”

The warming streams, the dying forests, the vanishing elk — the apocalypse is ecological as much as human.
Memory as Home
“I lift my head off the pillow / I see the frost the moon. / Lowering my head I think of home.”

Home is not a place — it's a person, a memory, a promise. Hig carries Melissa, Jasper, and the life before in every breath.
Violence & Compassion
“Try to do the right thing. Circumstance intervenes.”

Hig kills because he must, but never without cost. The novel refuses to resolve the tension — both cruelty and tenderness are real.
Found Family
By the end, Hig has a family again.

Cima, Pops, and even Bangley become something like a household — an apocalyptic parody of Norman Rockwell, but real.
Who We Become

A Spectrum, Not a Verdict

The novel refuses to say humans are fundamentally good or fundamentally cruel. Instead it lines its characters up along a spectrum and asks what circumstance — and choice — does to each of them.

The Flight Log

Past the Point of No Return

Hig's route across Colorado in three books — from the fortified perimeter at Erie, through grief and ambush, to a hidden canyon and back again.

✦ The Beast's Route
Erie — The Coke Truck — The Mountains — Grand Junction — The Canyon — Home
Book One
Life on the Perimeter
  • Hig and Bangley hold the Erie airport — Hig flies patrol in the Beast, Bangley mans the sniper tower, and an uneasy alliance keeps them both alive.
  • Hig secretly runs supplies to a Mennonite community dying of the blood disease, against Bangley's warnings.
  • A mysterious radio transmission reaches him from Grand Junction — a voice he puts off chasing for years.
  • On a last hunting trip into the mountains, Jasper dies in his sleep. Hig is left with nothing but the stars.
Book Two
The Journey West
  • Grieving and unmoored, Hig is ambushed by nine marauders on the plains — Bangley saves him with mortars fired from the perimeter.
  • Hig finally flies past his "point of no return," discovering a hidden box canyon with a stone house, a garden, and cattle.
  • He meets Cima, a doctor, and her father Pops, a former Navy SEAL — hostile at first, then cautiously trusting.
  • Hig and Cima fall into a tender relationship. Pops reveals the canyon's creek is drying up; the three plan to leave together.
Book Three
Return and Reckoning
  • The radio transmission from Grand Junction turns out to be a trap — an elderly couple luring pilots into a cable strung across the runway. Pops kills them.
  • Back at Erie, they find Bangley's house destroyed — he's alive behind a tool chest, having fought off fourteen attackers alone. Cima nurses him back.
  • The four become a household — Hig, Bangley, Cima, and Pops — an apocalyptic version of a family.
  • Unidentified jets, crews speaking Arabic, pass overhead. The novel ends open — other survivors, other threats, the world still turning.
The Final Word

When Will I Be Home?

The novel closes with Li Shang-Yin's poem — a meditation on longing, reunion, and the hope that burns even in the darkest night.

When Will I Be Home?
— Li Shang-Yin (Tang Dynasty)
When will I be home? I don't know.
In the mountains, in the rainy night,
The Autumn lake is flooded.
Someday we will be back together again.
We will sit in the candlelight by the West window.
And I will tell you how I remembered you
Tonight on the stormy mountain.
“The love is the creek bed and the pain fills it. Fills it every day with tears.”
— Hig, on the nature of grief
