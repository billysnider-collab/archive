PART ONE

What was removed, and why

You had a robotic right hemicolectomy. The surgeon removed the right side of your large intestine — the cecum and the ascending colon — along with a small piece of your small intestine, called the ileum.

The reason: you had a colon polyp that was either too large or too awkwardly placed to be taken out safely during a routine colonoscopy. Surgery was the best way to remove it completely.

PART TWO

What the pathologist saw by eye

Before the operation, the surgeon marked the spot with a small ink “tattoo” on the colon so the polyp’s location could be found again.

When the pathologist opened the removed tissue, no mass, ulcer or polyp was left to find. That usually means the polyp was very small, was removed completely, or had already been partly taken out during the earlier colonoscopy.

PART THREE

What they saw under the microscope

This is the most important line in the report. It reads:

“Benign colonic mucosa with focal hyperplastic changes.”

Benign
Non-cancerous.
Focal hyperplastic changes
In one small area, the cells were growing a little faster than usual — but they are ordinary, harmless cells. This is a very common finding. It is not cancer, and it is not pre-cancer.

The pathologist located and examined 11 lymph nodes near the section of colon that was removed. All 11 were clear. That confirms nothing had spread beyond the colon wall — and given the benign result, there was nothing there to spread in the first place.
